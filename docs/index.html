Why It Exists

The dash was great. The maps weren't my choice.

Suzuki Ride Connect only puts directions on the dash when you navigate inside Suzuki's own app, with Mappls maps. I plan every ride in Google Maps: my saved places, its traffic, its routes.

So I built the bridge.

Zuki Nav reads the directions Google Maps already shows on your phone and sends them to the console in the dash's own Bluetooth language: arrow, distance, ETA and distance left. Then it kept growing from real rides on a Gixxer SF 250: trips, fuel range, call and WhatsApp alerts, and a proper live dash panel in the app.

It's free, open source, and built by a rider for riders.

Features

Everything the dash can show, from the apps you already use.

Start a route in Google Maps and ride. Zuki Nav does the rest in the background.

Google Maps Turn-By-Turn

The turn arrow, distance to the turn, ETA and distance left on the dash, five times a second. Roundabouts and U-turns follow your traffic side.

Live Dash Panel

Speed gauge, next turn, fuel cells, ODO and both trip meters on your phone, live from the bike. The last readings stay when the ignition is off.

Mileage And Range

Spots refills when the fuel bars go up, works out km/l and cost per km from full tank to full tank, and learns km per bar to show how far you can still go.

Every Ride Saved

Ignition on to ignition off: distance, moving time, average and top speed, and where you started and ended. Star, rename or share any ride.

Call And Message Alerts

Phone and WhatsApp calls with the caller's name, new WhatsApp and SMS messages, and an option to skip bank, OTP and promo SMS.

Your Welcome

The dash greets you by name when it connects, with an optional spinning-arrow startup animation and a short greeting of your own.

Dash Test

Show any of the 64 turn icons on your dash and mark which look right, so every model can be checked and shared back.

Make It Yours

Dark, Light and true-black AMOLED themes, eight accent colours or your wallpaper's colours, and a rider avatar.

Just Works

Connects on its own when the ignition turns on, saves battery while the bike is parked, and updates itself from GitHub releases.

How It's Different

Zuki Nav and Suzuki Ride Connect

Both talk to the same dash over Bluetooth. The difference is where your directions come from, and what's built around them.

Setup

  1. Download the APK from the latest release and install it.
  2. Grant Nearby Devices and Notification Access when Setup asks.
  3. Close Suzuki Ride Connect, turn the ignition on and tap your bike to pair.
  4. Start a route in Google Maps and ride. The arrows appear on the dash.
